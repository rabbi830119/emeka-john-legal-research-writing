Article excerpt: What is a non-disclosure agreement?

A non-disclosure agreement, or NDA, is a contract that keeps shared information private. Businesses use it when they need to show sensitive material to someone else, such as a supplier, an investor or a new employee, without risking that it spreads.

A good NDA answers four questions. What counts as confidential? Who may see it? How long must it stay secret? And what happens if someone breaks the promise? If any of these is left vague, the agreement is harder to rely on.

Watch for two common problems. The first is a definition of "confidential information" so broad that it covers everything, including facts already public. The second is no end date. Most NDAs work best with a clear period, often two to five years, and a way to return or destroy the information afterwards.

Contract summary: one-page example

Document: Equipment Rental Agreement (sample). Parties: Lessor and Lessee.

Term: 12 months from the start date, renewable by written agreement. Payment: monthly rent due on the 5th, with a late fee after 7 days.

Key obligations: the Lessee keeps the equipment in good condition and reports damage within 48 hours. The Lessor handles major repairs.

Ending the contract: either side may end it with 30 days' written notice. Risks to check: the late fee rate and who pays for damage not caused by normal use.
